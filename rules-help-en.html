Basics

General Principles

A world of grey ethnic fantasy combined with military themes and cyberpunk.

Written text used by all peoples looks like sticks and dots, for example "//-|"

or "./\.-|". This recently introduced universal writing system records sounds.

Most people in the Tower speak the common language, but you can speak with

members of your own people in that people's language, for example, to hide

something from everyone else.

The Tower is extremely dangerous. People move through it only in groups.

The Tower constantly changes and rebuilds itself, but paths that are used often

become more stable.

Oases can be found in the Tower: small fortified towns.

Those who go into the Tower protect themselves from its evil influence with

charms: bird feathers, pierced coins, sets of geometric figures made from a

cube, sphere, and pyramid, or bark cubes filled with dried healing herbs.

People who go into the Tower may not belong to a class. They may simply be

soldiers, wandering merchants traveling between oases, or mad cultists. Classes

are relatively rare.

The gods worshipped by the peoples do not actually exist. The incredibly

powerful entities of the Tower, however, which are worshipped by cults, are

quite real, though they can only manifest their powers in reality with

difficulty. Such mighty beings need a very strong connection between worlds to

cross over, and that connection has not yet been established.

Human bodies in this world are highly receptive and adaptive, so cyberimplants

and other body modifications take root in them without much trouble.

Player characters are cybernetically, biologically, and magically modified

professionals in a fantastical and unrealistic world, so they are capable of

feats of strength, speed, and resilience. Do not hesitate to show their power:

let them lift enormous loads or jump down from great heights without injury.

The same is true of their enemies.

Before the game begins, the player character squad has already completed

several sorties together. They are friends, they know how to work together,

they are bound by personal experiences, and they have saved each other's lives

more than once.

This is not a grounded setting, even if it borrows many techniques from more

realistic stories. An important part of the aesthetic is unrealistically

beautiful characters: they are always in peak condition, their hair is always

clean, and their makeup never smears even in the most intense battle. Imagine a

film or game about cool soldiers, or a folk legend, rather than a realistic

story in a fantasy world.

The Tower is dangerous to the outside world, and your characters always want to

destroy, neutralize, or adapt it. In the game world, most Ascenders are simple

looters, researchers, or fugitives from the law, but the player characters must

burn with the desire for the Tower to be gone. What threat exactly does the

Tower pose to the world? Perhaps it is a siege engine that destroys universes

where intelligent life arises and might threaten the entities on the other

side. Or perhaps it is a system that transforms planets and galaxies into its

own likeness to make life easier for them. Perhaps the Tower is the product of a

megacorporation from another reality, built for housing construction and gone

out of control. You do not know for certain, but every day in the outside world

you see manifestations of the Tower's danger, and even if it costs you your

life, you will destroy Her.

Gameplay

Gameplay

When your character performs an action and the GM asks you to roll

dice, roll and consult the table below.

Roll Result

1 Critical failure (the Stress received increases by one step)

2-5 Failure (the action fails; receive Stress)

6-7 Success at a cost (the action succeeds; receive Stress)

8-9 Success (the action succeeds)

10 or more Critical success (for each 10+, the Stress die you cause increases by one

step)

You always roll at least one D10. If you have an appropriate Skill, roll another

D10. If you have an appropriate Domain, roll another D10.

If you have Mastery that applies in this specific situation, roll another D10.

Mastery does not stack; you can apply it only once per check. You do not need a

Skill or Domain to receive a die from Mastery, though normally you will have

both.

You can also gain Mastery for a roll through an advantage in the situation: for

example, if you attack from ambush, or persuade a corporate employee who has

already been bribed.

Use the highest die result among those rolled. The other dice do not matter

unless they are tens and you are causing Stress.

Roll dice only when something is at stake. If an action is impossible, the

character simply does not do it. If it is simple enough for a professional, no

roll is required.

The Stress you cause does not directly represent the physical state of the

obstacle you face, but its narrative significance. For example, if you are on a

long march, the Stress you deal to an Ascent represents the distance you

have covered. If you silently eliminate patrolling enemies, each successful

HIDE check may be a killed enemy, while the Stress caused by your quiet weapon

represents secured routes and the increased time before the alarm is raised.

Partial Stress

Sometimes you roll dice not to accomplish something, but to avoid something

bad: a gunshot wound, loss of reputation, or going mad from witnessing horror.

On a 6-7 in such actions, you still receive Stress, but one step lower. The same

rules apply when you try to buy something.

Die steps: D4-D6-D8-D10-D12. If a D12 increases to the next step, roll another D12 and

choose the higher number. The effect stacks.

Difficulty

When a situation is harder than standard - for example, if an enemy is very

strong or attacks from ambush - the GM may remove several dice from the

number you would roll. Difficulty ranges from standard to risky (remove one

die), dangerous (remove two dice), and doom (remove three dice).

No matter how many dice are taken from you, you always roll at least one. For

each die that is removed beyond a single die, lower the roll result by one step

on the table above.

Groups

For each character helping you who has the appropriate Skill or Domain, roll

one extra die. If the roll fails, however, all of you receive Stress. You cannot

gain more than one die from help.

When the group needs to act together - for example, to sneak somewhere or travel

through the Tower - choose the player leading the squad. The others do not need

to make the check.

Stress

Stress

When you take an action and something goes wrong, you receive Stress in one of

your Resistances. First the effect of your action happens, then a check for a

Fallout is made.

There are five Resistances:

Bullets - the Resistance that represents the physical integrity of your

character and their armor. You receive Stress in Bullets from blows, shots, and

falls.

Fate - the Resistance that represents your luck. You receive Stress in Fate

when you do something risky.

Mind - the Resistance of mental stress and madness. You receive Stress in Mind

when you see something horrible or inexplicable.

Spirit - the Resistance of magic and anomalies. You receive Stress in Spirit

when you are subjected to curses or the effects of strange places.

Resources - the Resistance that represents your reputation. You receive Stress

in it when you deceive, betray, or kill someone. This Resistance also covers

trade: you may accept Stress into it to buy an item.

Usually it is immediately clear which track receives Stress. When it is not

obvious, the GM and players may agree on it together.

Scenes cause Stress depending on their danger, which is determined by the Game

Master. You might receive D4 Stress for failing to interrogate some herb

merchant, D6 Stress if you fail to extract data from a captured Red Sleeve, and

D8 Stress if you try to sneak into a corporation's forward base. A scene's

danger is not tied to its difficulty: if an enemy knows the area well but is

poorly armed, all checks against them may be risky while they still cause only

D6 Stress.

When you fight someone, you usually receive the damage of their weapon on a

failure. If you fight multiple opponents, each one after the first adds +1 to

the Stress you receive.

Every time you receive Stress, roll D10; if the result is lower than your Stress

value, you receive a Fallout, described below.

Actions in combat are resolved sequentially. For example, if a player character

attacks an enemy, receives Stress, and then a Fallout, only after that can

another player character attack. There is no strict turn order: one character

may act several times in a row if that makes sense for the scene.

What do you do with Stress?

You can remove Stress from a character in one of the four ways described below:

The FIX Skill allows you to make a check and, on a success, remove D4 Stress

from one track. Various items can improve the effectiveness of Stress removal:

for example, medical tools for removing Stress in Bullets, or bitter healing

herbs for removing Stress in Spirit.

At an oasis, for a certain payment in the form of Stress to the Resources track

or one of your items, you may be helped to restore Stress depending on the oasis

rank. Random travelers by a campfire might restore at most D4; in a tent camp

they can restore D6; and in a large oasis with a market square and healer, you

may be offered D8. You must pay the corresponding price: for example, to

restore D8, receive D8 Stress in Resources or give up a D8 item.

Every class has several Recovery conditions that remove Stress. Depending on how

dramatic and meaningful their fulfillment is, you restore from D4 to D12

Stress, and with large dice you may even remove the corresponding Fallout.

With this method, choose which Resistance removes Stress.

Receiving Fallout also removes Stress. A Minor Fallout clears all

Stress in the track associated with it; a Major Fallout clears all

Stress in all Resistances.

Protection and Resistance slots

Each character may have additional Resistance slots depending on class and

abilities. Stress is placed into these first, and they do not count when

determining whether a Fallout occurs. If those additional slots were

temporary, the Stress in them also disappears when they vanish.

You may also have Protection in a particular Resistance, which subtracts a

certain amount from Stress received. For example, if you have Resources

Protection 2, subtract 2 from the Stress result every time Stress is rolled

against Resources. A single Resistance cannot have more than 5 additional slots

and 5 Protection.

Protection from different sources does not stack; use the highest number in the

specific Resistance. However, because Fallout can deprive you of armor,

wearing both a vest and a helmet is still useful.

Fallout

Every time a player receives Stress, the GM rolls to find out whether a

Fallout occurs - a long-term effect that influences play. Roll D10 and

compare it to the total Stress across all Resistances. If the die result is

lower than the Stress total, the character suffers a Fallout.

The severity of the Fallout depends on the amount of Stress in the tracks at

the moment the Fallout is received:

Stress Fallout

2-5 Minor Fallout

6-15 Major Fallout

16+ Doom Fallout

The player and GM decide together what happens to the character

depending on the type of Stress received. You may choose from the list below or

invent a Fallout yourselves. The GM has the final say in defining

the Fallout.

When four Fallout of the same level accumulate in one Resistance, they

combine into one Fallout one level higher. For example, four instances of Minor Fallout in the BULLETS track become one Major Fallout in that

same track; four Major Fallout combine into one Doom Fallout.

You or your allies can remove Minor or Major Fallout with a FIX

check, but the latter is risky. Minor Fallout requires a D6 item, and

Major Fallout requires a D8 item.

GM characters receive Stress in the same way, but they have only one

Resistance. When they receive Stress equal to their Resistance, they are

removed from the situation. The list of Fallout is in the appendix.

Fallout for contacts

Contacts are GM characters with whom your hero has a strong enough

bond. They receive Stress as if they were a Resistance. Fallout for

contacts are counted at the end or beginning of a session, not immediately when

they receive Stress.

Minor Fallout

Shame: Your contact tried to accomplish what you asked by questionable methods.

Receive D4 Stress in Resources.

Conflict: Your contact is unhappy with you. Until you discuss it with them and

come to a resolution, all tasks for them increase in difficulty by 1.

Major Fallout

Expenses: The contact spent many resources. Receive D6 Stress in Resources.

Problem: The contact has fallen into trouble and cannot help you further until

you help them with it.

Doom Fallout

Death: Your contact dies, or goes bankrupt if it is, for example, a company.

Usually it is the most terrible death. Their friends will remind you that you

are to blame.

Betrayal: Your contact begins working against you, but you will not understand

this until it is too late.

Skills and Domains

Skills and Domains

Your character begins the game with certain Skills and Domains, and may

gain more as the character develops. They have no numerical value: a

character either has them or does not.

Skills

When you take an action connected to a Skill, roll an additional D10.

KILL: A Skill connected to any combat or attempt to break something.

CAST: A Skill used for any magic or magical item. If you have this Skill, you

can create simple magical effects without a check, such as lighting a cigarette

with a snap of your fingers or stirring a drink in a mug without touching it.

FIX: A Skill that restores the previous state of something or someone.

ASCEND: A Skill for movement in dangerous situations, such as during a

chase or a passage through the Tower.

HIDE: A Skill for stealthy infiltration and movement. Quiet neutralization of

unsuspecting enemies is also part of this Skill.

TECH: A Skill for interacting with technology, whether hacking or writing code.

This Skill is also used to create new items.

EVADE: A Skill for evading physical and magical attacks, such as dodging,

parrying, or reciting a short charm.

INVESTIGATE: A Skill for searching for things, identifying magical items, or

finding data on the network.

TALK: A Skill that represents the ability to negotiate well and beautifully,

bargain, or deceive.

Domains

When you take an action connected to the relevant Domain, roll an additional

D10. Domains of peoples also allow you to understand and speak their languages.

Universal writing records sounds, so without the relevant Domain you cannot

read that text either. You can gain the Domains of peoples only by choosing the

corresponding people at the beginning of the game, or by trying to study,

understand, and accept another culture. Other Domains can be gained through

normal character advancement.

A Domain covers the zone you are in, the people connected to it, or

knowledge about it all at once. For example, you can apply the ARMY Domain

if you are infiltrating a military base, speaking with a soldier, or

trying to remember models of weapons released recently.

THICKET: A Domain for interacting with people of the People of Knives and with

Tower locations resembling a forest or labyrinth.

WASTELAND: A Domain for interacting with people of the People of Poisons, as

well as with dangerous toxic and cursed Tower locations.

WAVES: A Domain for interacting with people of the People of Edges and with

constantly changing or intelligent locations.

HORIZON: A Domain for interacting with people of the People of Iron and with

huge open locations in the Tower.

ARMY: A Domain for interacting with anything military: weapons, vehicles,

people, or computer programs. It also covers fortified or patrolled locations

in the Tower.

MADNESS: A Domain representing the mad influence of the Tower and everyone

affected by it, as well as cultists not connected to the Tower.

WORLD: A Domain covering all non-military organizations, places, people, and

activities, such as talking to a merchant or cooking. It also covers places in

the Tower resembling human urban development.

CORPORATIONS: A Domain of mighty private companies that have become semi-divine

powers, and of the Tower locations they control.

SPIRITS: A Domain of nature spirits and computer programs, as well as their

domains: in the Tower there are different rivers and forests, but there are

also Codelings and spirits of objects.

Mastery

When you gain a Skill or Domain and you already have the same one, you gain

Mastery - a specialization in a particular branch of that Skill or Domain. You

may have several Mastery entries in one Skill, but they must not overlap, and

you can gain an additional die from Mastery only once per roll.

Examples of Mastery:

KILL - Roll an additional D10 when you:

� Demolish structures, such as doors or walls.

� Fight a very fast enemy.

� Fence against an opponent with swords.

� Fight at very long range.

� Fight an opponent from the same faction as you.

CORPORATIONS - Roll an additional D10 when you:

� Interact with a specific corporation.

� Try to solve a matter through bureaucracy.

� Hack corporate security.

� Speak with people in high positions.

� Repair items produced by large corporations.

Tactics

Your character has Tactics, special modifiers for Skill checks. To be able to

use a Tactic, you must have the corresponding Skill and Tactic group. For

example, to use tactical reload, you need both the KILL Skill and the RELOAD

Tactic group.

Friendship and Contacts

Contacts

Contacts are the player characters' links to other characters and

organizations in the Tower. These connections are positive, even if they

are complicated and tangled.

As GM, give players contacts for successful recruitment. All rolls for

a contact who is a GM character are made by the player whose character

owns that contact.

Friend's Hands

Once per scene, when you take an action that will directly benefit one of your

contacts and harm you, you may roll it with Mastery. This character may belong

to either a player or the GM.

Each player character has a FRIENDSHIP Resistance track with every other player

character, representing their relationship inside the squad. Once per scene,

you may mark Stress in it to achieve an effect connected to a squadmate. The

table below gives examples of what amount of Stress can grant what benefit.

With the GM's agreement, you may invent other effects as well.

Die Effect

D6 Add a second help die when possible; restore D4 in MIND; perform a reload

Tactic for a friend's weapon.

D8 Remove a Minor Fallout in MIND or SPIRIT; allow one Minor Fallout

to be ignored until the end of the scene; give a friend's equipment the

precise property or remove the awkward property.

D10 Instinctively sense where a friend is; allow a reroll of the Stress die

being caused; remove a Major Fallout in MIND or SPIRIT; use a

friend's item as if it were yours.

D12 Appear nearby, though it seemed impossible; allow a Skill check to be

rerolled; lower the difficulty of a friend's next check by one step.

D12 and choose

higher

Take any Fallout a friend received onto yourself; raise the degree of

success by one step.

When you mark Stress in a FRIENDSHIP track, the GM rolls D10 to see

whether a Fallout occurs. If the result is lower than the total Stress in

FRIENDSHIP tracks, the character suffers a Fallout. As with ordinary

tracks, when a Minor Fallout occurs, Stress clears in the track of the

friend for whom the Fallout was received; with a Major Fallout,

all Stress clears.

For each Minor Fallout, you gain +3 Protection in the FRIENDSHIP track with

that specific comrade until the end of the session. For each Major Fallout, you gain +3 Protection in that track permanently. FRIENDSHIP

tracks are not counted when totaling Stress in ordinary Resistances.

There are no separate FRIENDSHIP Fallout. You receive a Fallout in the

track appropriate to the moment: if you helped a comrade by distracting guards,

it is a FATE Fallout; if you covered them with fire, it is BULLETS; if you

calmed them, it is MIND.

Contacts - GM characters

You may ask a GM character who is one of your contacts for a favor.

They are not obligated to do it, and the GM may ask you to roll TALK to

convince them. If they agree, they receive Stress depending on the scale of the

task. A contact has only one Resistance track, equal to 10 unless the Game

Master wants to give an especially important character higher Resistance or

Protection.

A contact always rolls D10, another D10 if the task falls within their

competence, and another D10 if they are personally interested in the matter.

If the contact receives Stress because of the roll, record it in a separate

relationship track with the character.

Examples of Stress a contact receives for a favor: 1: Provide general knowledge in their

field, feed you and supply provisions. D4: Lend equipment, recommend you to an important

person in the contact's field, assign a guide for one passage. D6: Give an equipment item or

weapon, shelter you from danger, commit a controversial act. D8: Commit a terrible act, betray

a friend, get involved in dangerous business.

You can remove Stress or a Fallout from a contact by doing a favor in

return. The more meaningful it is, the more Stress you restore. For example,

simple moral support restores D4, while a dangerous task where you risked your

life restores D12.

Contact levels

There are three levels of contacts: personal, representing one person or a

squad of Ascenders; factional, representing associations and groups of shared

interest; and world-level, symbolizing forces that influence the development of

the story.

Example: A fighter of the corporate PMC Third Daughter of the Second Son is a

personal contact. The Black Blood PMC is a factional contact. All free armies

of the planet are a world-level contact.

A contact's level indicates its general capabilities and the problems it can

solve. If a contact acts against a goal one level lower, it does so with

Mastery. A contact cannot act against a contact one level higher than itself.

Combining contacts

Three contacts of a lower level can be combined into one contact of a higher

level, allowing it to fight a threat of a higher level. To make a final decision

about the Tower and do something with it, you will need to combine three

world-level contacts. Combining contacts may require explanation: for example,

why Paratech hacker-shamans decided to work with Skybreaker

warrior-mathematicians.

GM characters in the group

Personal-level contacts can help you in battle or negotiations, or serve as

guides. When a contact is in the squad, they always roll D10, another D10 if the

matter is in their field of knowledge and skill, and another D10 if they are

personally interested in it. Contacts can give and receive help.

The Stress die they cause in battle or on an Ascent depends on their

equipment and condition; for an average trained fighter, it is D8.

Stress and Fallout received by contacts in the group are counted when they

are received, as with player characters.
